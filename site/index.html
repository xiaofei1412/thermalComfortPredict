Hybrid Building Thermal Control

Can a home use less heating electricity without increasing indoor temperature violations?

HBTC studies this question in a simulated single-zone home. Buildings store heat, so today’s setpoint can affect room temperature hours later. Weather and solar gains also shift when heating is needed.

Two paths to heating control

01 · Plan with a thermal model

Model predictive control

A gray-box thermal model represents heat flow and storage. A learned residual corrects its temperature and power predictions. MPC uses weather forecasts to evaluate candidate heating plans, screens them against temperature margins and setpoint limits, then applies the first action before planning again.

When stored heat and solar gains are enough, MPC can reduce heating early. A recovery rule handles conditions outside the model’s range and steps with no feasible plan.

1.62%–9.49% less heating electricity

Across nine held-out date and forecast combinations in the simulated home, MPC used less electricity than the recovery rule with no increase in temperature violations.

02 · Learn a setpoint policy

Reinforcement learning

A policy learns to adjust heating setpoints through repeated simulation. Its reward penalizes electricity use, temperature violations, and setpoint changes. SAC, PPO, and TD3 learn adjustments directly. Rule-anchored residual SAC learns a bounded correction to a rule-based action.

The study uses three seeds per method and a fixed training budget. Evaluation aligns the weather forecasts and setpoint limits available to the policies, MPC, and the reference rule.

Savings in some development windows

Some SAC and TD3 policies saved electricity in warmer windows while meeting temperature criteria. Results varied by weather and seed, so the RL study concluded at the development stage.
